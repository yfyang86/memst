---

Dr. Patterson: "Good morning, James. I have your test results from last week's stress test and blood work. Please, have a seat."

James: "Is it bad news, doc?"

Dr. Patterson: "It's concerning, but manageable. Your LDL cholesterol is at 180 mg/dL - that's well above the optimal level of 100. Your blood pressure was 145/92 during the stress test. Both indicate hypertension and hyperlipidemia."

Linda: "His father had a heart attack at 60. Should we be worried?"

Dr. Patterson: "Family history is a significant risk factor. Combined with James's sedentary work as a software developer and his smoking history - even though he quit 3 years ago - his 10-year ASCVD risk score is 18.2%. That's intermediate risk."

James: "What does that mean exactly?"

Dr. Patterson: "It means you have about an 18% chance of a heart attack or stroke in the next 10 years if we don't intervene. The good news is we caught this early. I want to start you on atorvastatin 20mg daily for the cholesterol. That's a statin medication."

Linda: "Are there side effects?"

Dr. Patterson: "Some patients experience muscle aches, but it's generally well-tolerated. We'll monitor your liver function with blood tests every 6 months. I also want you to start lisinopril 10mg for the blood pressure. It's an ACE inhibitor."

James: "How long do I need to take these?"

Dr. Patterson: "Likely indefinitely, unless your numbers improve significantly with lifestyle changes. Speaking of which - diet and exercise are crucial. The Mediterranean diet has the strongest evidence for cardiovascular health. Limit red meat, increase fish, nuts, olive oil. And I need you walking 30 minutes daily, minimum."

James: "I can do that."

Dr. Patterson: "I also want you to wear this ambulatory blood pressure monitor for 24 hours. It will take readings every 30 minutes. We need to confirm you don't have white coat hypertension."

Linda: "When should we follow up?"

Dr. Patterson: "Come back in 6 weeks. We'll repeat the blood work and check your blood pressure response to the medication. If everything looks good, we can space appointments to every 6 months. But James - and I can't stress this enough - take the medications exactly as prescribed. Don't skip doses because you feel fine. High blood pressure is called the silent killer for a reason."

James: "I understand, doctor. Thank you."

Dr. Patterson: "Any other questions? No? The nurse will give you the prescriptions and schedule your follow-up. We'll get through this together, James."
